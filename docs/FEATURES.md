## 1. Reading the Bible

### 1.1 The reading page

- **Continuous scrolling** — chapters load as you scroll, so you can read from Genesis 1 straight through without tapping "next chapter". Scrolling backwards loads the previous chapter the same way.
- **Verse layout** — three ways to see the text:
  - Each verse on its own line, the classic study layout.
  - Flowing paragraphs with small verse numbers, closest to a printed Bible.
  - Flowing paragraphs with no verse numbers at all, for distraction-free reading.
- **Word wrap** — on by default. Turning it off lets long lines run off the side instead of wrapping, which suits poetry and interlinear rows.
- **Your place is remembered** — the app reopens to the translation, book, and chapter you were last reading, even after closing it completely.

### 1.2 What appears in the text

- **Words of Jesus in red** — red-letter text, shown correctly in dark, light, and sepia themes. Can be turned off.
- **Section headings** — the small pericope titles between verses ("The Sermon on the Mount"). Can be turned off.
- **Footnotes** — small blue numbers in the text. Tap one to read the translator's note.
- **Cross-reference markers** — same small numbers, in grey rather than blue, when the note points to another passage instead of explaining wording. The app tells the two apart automatically.
- **Bold and italic** — preserved from translations that use them, including the italics some Bibles use for supplied words.
- **Art icons** — a small icon next to verses that a painting depicts. Tap it to open the artwork. Can be turned off.
- **Place-name underlines** — an optional faint dotted underline under multi-word place names like "Red Sea" or "Abel Beth Maacah", so you can see which words act as a single place when tapped. Off by default, and needs the encyclopedia pack.
- **Themed titles** — book and chapter titles carry a subtle colored shadow matching the book's category. Can be turned off.

### 1.3 Book introductions

Every book opens with an introduction panel covering who wrote it, when, and what it is about. It can be opened at any time from the book title, and closing it returns you to exactly where you were reading.

### 1.4 Book of Enoch

Two complete English editions are included and read like any other book:

- Robert Henry Charles, 1917
- Richard Laurence, 1821

## 2. Getting Around

### 2.1 The book and chapter picker

- **All 66 books, grouped and color-coded** into ten categories — Pentateuch, Historical, Wisdom, Major Prophets, Minor Prophets, Gospels, Acts, Pauline Epistles, General Epistles, and Eschaton — so you can find a book by shape and color rather than by reading every name.
- **Chapter grid** — pick any chapter directly, no scrolling through a long list.
- **Books you can't open are hidden** — if the translation you're in is New Testament only, the Old Testament books don't appear.

### 2.2 Moving between passages

- **Back button** — steps back through the passages you've visited, like a browser's back button. It only appears when there's somewhere to go back to.
- **Tap any reference to follow it** — references inside cross-references, commentaries, and encyclopedia articles are all live links. The verse you land on is marked in its book's category color so you can spot where to start reading. The mark stays until you follow another link or step away by hand, and it comes back if you leave the chapter and return.
- **Reference shorthand is understood** — "Ex 20:21", "Am 5:18-20", or a bare "3:14" while you're already in a book all resolve correctly. Common abbreviations and alternate book spellings are recognised.
- **The toolbar follows your scrolling** — as you scroll into a new chapter, the book and chapter shown in the toolbar update to match, without yanking the page around.

## 3. Translations

- **Translation picker** — switch between every translation you have installed from the toolbar.
- **Switching keeps your place** — you stay on the same book and chapter. If that book doesn't exist in the translation you picked (switching to a Greek New Testament while in Genesis, say), the app moves you to the nearest book that does.
- **Daily drivers** — set a preferred translation for each combination of language and testament: English Old Testament, English New Testament, Hebrew Old Testament, Greek Old Testament, Greek New Testament, and Hebrew New Testament. Cross-references, search, and other features then open in the right translation for the passage automatically, rather than always defaulting to one.

## 4. Interlinear (Greek & Hebrew)

Shows the original-language text with helper layers stacked under each word. It applies only when you have a Greek or Hebrew translation open.

### 4.1 Turning it on

A toolbar button toggles it while you read. The same controls also live in Settings, with a live preview using John 1:1 so you can see the effect before applying it.

### 4.2 Presets

- **Minimal** — English meaning only.
- **Study** — English meaning plus Strong's number.
- **Scholar** — everything: meaning, pronunciation, dictionary form, Strong's number, and grammar.
- **Custom** — appears automatically when you change layers by hand.

### 4.3 The layers

- **English gloss** — the English equivalent under each word. Always on; it's the point of an interlinear.
- **Transliteration** — how the word is pronounced, in English letters.
- **Lemma** — the dictionary form of the word, as you'd look it up.
- **Strong's number** — the standard reference number for the word.
- **Parsing** — the grammar: part of speech, tense, case, gender, number.

## 5. Word Study

Tap any word in the text to open a full study panel for it. Works on English words and on Greek and Hebrew words.

### 5.1 For Greek and Hebrew words

- **Entry information** — the word, its transliteration, its dictionary form, and its Strong's number.
- **Short definition** — the quick answer.
- **Full definition** — the complete lexicon entry.
- **KJV usage** — every way the King James Version rendered this word, which shows its range of meaning at a glance.
- **Derivation** — the root the word comes from.
- **Inflection forms** — the other forms this word takes across the text.
- **Occurrences** — every verse in the Bible using this word, so you can see how it's actually used rather than only how it's defined.
- **Related words** — synonyms and words sharing a root.

### 5.2 For English words

- **Dictionary definitions** — grouped by part of speech.
- **Plurals and variants are handled** — tapping "waters" finds "water"; tapping a verb form finds its base form.
- **People are recognised** — tapping a name opens that person's entry with the verses they appear in, rather than a dictionary definition of the word.

### 5.3 Grammar in plain English

Morphology codes are translated out of shorthand. Instead of `V-PAI-3S` you read "Verb, Present, Active, Indicative, Third person, Singular", and instead of `HNcmsa`, "Hebrew, Noun, common, masculine, singular, absolute". All three coding systems the packs use are covered — Greek, Hebrew/Aramaic, and the STEPBible part-of-speech format.

## 6. Bible Encyclopedia

Full articles from the *International Standard Bible Encyclopedia* — a complete, public-domain Bible encyclopedia, included free and available offline.

### 6.1 Opening an article

Select a word or phrase in the text and choose **More Info**. The app works out whether you tapped a place, a person, or a general subject and opens the matching article.

### 6.2 What an article shows

- **Overview** — the summary: what kind of thing it is, its other names, and where it appears.
- **Article** — the full encyclopedia text, broken into a collapsible table of contents. Long entries stay navigable instead of becoming a wall of text, and repeated section headings are handled so the contents list stays clean.
- **Verses** — every verse mentioning the subject, grouped by book in Bible order, each one tappable.
- **Map** — for places, a pin at the location, when coordinates are known.

### 6.3 Browsing it like a book

You don't have to tap a word to get in. The encyclopedia has its own A–Z contents you can read through, with page turns, its own search, and entries you can star — see [23](#23-the-study-library).

### 6.4 Linked to the other works

Where an encyclopedia entry and a dictionary entry describe the same thing, the app connects them, so you can move between the short definition and the long article without searching again. The same goes for the topical index and the people entries — all four are one tap apart.

### 6.5 Place-name underlines

The encyclopedia pack also powers the optional dotted underlines described in [1.2](#12-what-appears-in-the-text), which mark multi-word place names so you can see what's tappable as a unit.

## 7. Commentaries

Fourteen classic commentaries, all public domain, all offline.

### 7.1 The commentators

NET Bible Notes · Adam Clarke · John Wesley · John Calvin · KingComments · A.T. Robertson · Albert Barnes · E.W. Bullinger · Family Bible Notes · Abbott · Thomas Aquinas (Catena Aurea) · Matthew Henry · Jamieson-Fausset-Brown · Charles Spurgeon

Each author has a fixed color and a two-letter badge, so once you learn that green is Clarke you can pick their notes out at a glance anywhere in the app.

### 7.2 Reading commentary

- **Markers in the text** — verses with commentary carry small colored badges, one per author.
- **Filter by author** — pick which commentators you want to see. Everyone else's markers disappear, so you're not wading through fourteen opinions when you want one.
- **Open in its own pane** — commentary can sit beside the text rather than covering it, so you can read both together.
- **References are clickable** — every Bible reference inside a commentary is a live link, including shorthand like "v. 14" that depends on the passage you're in. They're colored to match the book being referenced.

### 7.3 Anchor sync

Turn on the anchor and the commentary pane follows the verse you're reading. If you scroll the commentary somewhere else, the app notices you've drifted and stops fighting you; tap the anchor again to snap back to where you're reading.

You can also drop **checkpoints** on verses you're working through. They show as amber marks in the Bible text so you can see which verses you've already studied in the commentary.

## 8. Cross-References

Built on the *Treasury of Scripture Knowledge*, the classic cross-reference collection.

- **Toggle markers on or off** — a toolbar button shows small gold diamonds on the keywords in a verse that have cross-references.
- **Tap a marker** to see every passage connected to that word, each one a link.
- **Shorthand is resolved for you** — a reference written "3:14,15" or just "31" is understood relative to the book and chapter you're reading, and lands you in the right place.

## 9. Search

Two ways to search, depending on how hard the question is.

### 9.1 Quick search

Type in the toolbar box and results appear as you type, grouped by where they came from:

- **Bible** — verse text
- **Strong's** — original-language entries
- **Notes** — your own notes
- **Journal** — your journal entries
- **Saved verses**
- **Characters** — people in the Bible
- **Encyclopedia** — ISBE articles
- **Commentaries**

Typing a Strong's number straight into the box (`G26`, `H430`, `g0026`) jumps directly to that entry.

Results are grouped in a tree you can collapse, and each category is capped so one huge category can't bury the rest. Commentary search is heavy, so it runs only when you press Enter rather than on every keystroke.

### 9.2 Advanced search

For precise work, with built-in help on every option:

- **Match type** — contains anywhere, starts with, ends with, whole word only, word begins with, word ends with.
- **Must also contain** — narrow results to verses that include another term too.
- **Must NOT contain** — exclude verses containing a term.
- **Proximity** — find words near each other, within a distance you set.
- **Include plurals** — match "waters" when you searched "water".
- **Include synonyms** — widen to related words.
- **Case-insensitive** — on or off.
- **Show pronunciation** — display transliterations alongside original-language results.

Every one of these has a **?** button explaining what it does with worked examples.

## 10. Highlights & Notes

### 10.1 Highlighting

Select any verse or any single word and choose a style. Seven colors — yellow, green, orange, red, pink, purple, blue — in three styles:

- **Marker** — a highlighter-pen background. It's drawn with a hand-drawn wavy edge rather than a flat rectangle, and each verse always gets the same shape, so it looks like a real highlighter rather than a computer block. Highlights that wrap across lines get a proper swatch on each line.
- **Text color** — colors the words themselves instead of the background.
- **Underline** — solid, dashed, wavy, or boxed.

Verse highlights and single-word highlights are tracked separately, so you can highlight one word inside an already-highlighted verse.

### 10.2 Notes

- Attach a note to any verse.
- Verses with notes are marked, and tapping the mark opens the note.
- Notes are searchable from the main search box.

### 10.3 Saved verses

Bookmark verses you want to come back to and browse them in one list.

### 10.4 Selecting words

- **Tap a word** to select it.
- **Drag across words** to select a phrase.
- **Tap again to extend** a selection you've already made.

Selection snaps to whole words, so you never end up with half of one.

### 10.5 The selection menu

Selecting text brings up everything you can do to it:

**Dissect** (word study) · **Search** · **Map** (for places) · **Highlight** · **Save** · **Notes** · **Repeats**

The Map button only appears when what you selected is actually a place, and it opens the real map.

Two styles, switchable in Settings:

- **Ring** (default) — the options arc around the tapped word rather than sitting over it. There are deliberate gaps at the left and right, lined up with the line of text, so **the sentence you're reading stays readable straight through the menu**. Near the edge of the screen the word simply ends up in one of those gaps instead of the middle, and is still perfectly visible. The buttons sweep in one at a time.
- **Popup** — the older menu above or below the word, positioned so it never covers what you tapped.

## 11. Repeated Words

A study tool for tracking a word through the text — the digital version of circling every occurrence of "covenant" in a paper Bible.

- **Track up to seven words at once**, each in its own color: Amber, Sky, Mint, Rose, Violet, Coral, Teal.
- **Every occurrence lights up** — once a word is tracked, it's softly highlighted everywhere in the Bible, not just the chapter you're on.
- **Pills in the toolbar** — each tracked word gets a colored pill. The pill and the in-text highlight always match.
- **Counts per book** — see how many times your word occurs in the book you're reading.
- **Highlight All** — turn a tracked word into permanent saved highlights in one go.
- **Tracking survives restarts** — your tracked words are still there tomorrow.

The repeat colors are deliberately softer and more translucent than the saved-highlight colors, so this layer reads as temporary scratch work rather than something you've committed to.

## 12. Journal

- **One entry per day**, with a title and body.
- **Rich text editing** — formatting, not just plain text.
- **Bible references become links** — write "Romans 8:28" in an entry and it turns into a link you can tap later.
- **Calendar view** — see which days you've written on and jump to any of them.
- **Searchable** — journal entries appear in the main search results.
- **Keyboard shortcut** — press **J** anywhere in the app to open today's entry in a side pane.

## 13. Reading Plans & Progress

### 13.1 Ready-made plans

- **Bible in 1 Year**
- **New Testament in 90 Days**
- **Gospels in 30 Days**
- **Chronological Bible in 1 Year** — events in the order they happened rather than book order
- **Psalms & Proverbs**
- **Robertson Gospel Harmony** — the four Gospels woven into a single narrative, in 30, 60, 90, or 184 days (the last being one section per day)

### 13.2 Build your own

Pick the books, the number of days, and the order — canonical, chronological, or shuffled. Name it whatever you like.

### 13.3 Running a plan

- **Run several plans at once** — a year-long plan and a short study plan side by side, each tracked separately.
- **Today's reading** is surfaced for you, and if you're not caught up, the next unread day is.
- **Tick chapters off** as you read them. In Gospel Harmony plans you tick off passages rather than chapters.
- **Jump straight to the passage** from the plan.
- **Rename, archive, or delete** any plan. Deleting takes two taps, so you can't lose a year's progress by mistake.
- **A congratulations screen** when you finish.

### 13.4 Falling behind

If you miss days, the plan offers to catch you up two ways:

- **Even spread** — the missed reading is distributed across your remaining days.
- **Dedicated catch-up days** — the missed reading is grouped into its own days.

### 13.5 Progress and history

- **Reading streak** — consecutive days you've read.
- **Total chapters read** across all time.
- **Reading history** — what you read and when, kept per chapter.
- **Calendar view** of your reading days.
- **Progress syncs across devices** if you're signed in, and merges sensibly — a chapter ticked on your phone stays ticked when your tablet syncs, and vice versa.

## 14. Maps & Places

- **Map pane** — open a map beside the text.
- **Places in the verse you're reading** can be looked up directly.
- **Search for a place** by name.
- **Historical layers** — map layers for different biblical time periods, selectable by period or by year, so you can see the world as it was rather than as it is.
- **Places by type** — cities, regions, water features, and so on.
- **Works offline** — map tiles are stored on your device, so the map works with no connection.
- **Linked to the encyclopedia** — a place on the map connects to its encyclopedia article, and a place in an article connects to the map.

## 15. Art

- **Classical paintings of Bible scenes**, tied to the verses they depict.
- **Icons in the text** mark verses that have artwork. Tap to open.
- **Art pane** — browse all artwork, or everything for the chapter you're reading.
- **Search artwork** by title or subject.
- **Opens where it fits** — the art window docks to whichever screen edge suits your current orientation.

## 16. Read Aloud

The Bible read to you by an AI voice that runs **entirely on your device** — no account, no internet connection, and nothing you read is sent anywhere.

### 16.1 Voices

- **Standard (US English)** — the fuller-sounding voice, about 64 MB.
- **Compact (US English)** — about 30 MB, for tight storage.

Voices download once, on your say-so — the app never quietly pulls 60 MB in the background. You can also install your own voice from a file, including a cloned one.

### 16.2 Playing

- **Read this chapter aloud** from the toolbar.
- **Pause and resume.**
- **Jump to a verse** and start reading from there.
- **Speed control** from 0.8× to 1.5×.
- **Auto-advance** — keep reading into the next chapter automatically, or stop at the end of this one.
- **Keeps reading past the chapter you started in.** Reading is no longer tied to the chapter on screen: it carries on when you scroll away, close the panel, or lock the phone, and announces each new book and chapter as it reaches it.
- **Controls follow you.** Once reading starts, play and pause sit in the toolbar wherever you are in the app.
- **The app icon spins** while the voice is being generated, so you can tell the difference between thinking and stopped.

### 16.3 On the lock screen

The reading appears on your lock screen like any other audio: what's being read, which verse of how many, and working play, pause and skip buttons.

This is also what makes listening with the phone in your pocket work at all. A phone slows down web pages it thinks are idle, and a page registered as a media player is treated as one that should keep running.

### 16.4 Sleep timer

Set a timer and the reading fades out and stops — after a number of minutes, or at the end of the current chapter.

**It never navigates.** Where you fell asleep is where you wake up, so the next morning you carry on from the right place rather than from wherever the audio happened to stop.

### 16.5 Following along

Two independent effects — use either, both, or neither:

- **Highlight the verse being read** — tints the current verse. On by default.
- **Drifting glow** — a soft cloud of light that glides along the words as they're spoken. Off by default.

The glow is deliberately soft and a few words wide. The speech model hands back finished audio without telling the app exactly when each word is spoken, so the pace is an estimate. A sharp highlight on the wrong word looks broken; a blurred cloud is almost always covering the right word somewhere in its span, and reads as atmosphere rather than error.

### 16.6 Section headings

Optionally have section headings spoken before their verse. Off by default.

## 17. Audio

Separate from Read Aloud: playback of pre-recorded chapter audio, where a pack provides it.

- Play, pause, stop.
- Seek within the chapter.
- Auto-advance to the next chapter.

## 18. Panes & Windows

The app can show several things at once — Bible text, commentary, a map, your journal — arranged how you like.

### 18.1 Opening a window

Drag inward from any edge of the screen — top, left, right, or bottom — and a new window opens at the size you dragged to. Drag further for a bigger window.

The gesture is deliberately careful about not firing by accident: it waits until it can tell which direction you're actually moving before committing, and the bottom edge leaves a gap in the middle for the Android home gesture, so you don't open a window every time you try to leave the app.

### 18.2 What can go in a window

Bible text · Map · Notes · Word study · Commentaries · Journal · Art · Encyclopedia · Topical · People

Up to six windows at once. Each is resizable, and each remembers its own state — a second Bible window can sit on a different chapter from the main one.

The whole header bar is the resize grip, so there's no thin edge to hunt for. Windows open beside the text on a wide screen and beneath it on a phone, decided by which way there's room to spare rather than by screen size, so a portrait tablet behaves like a portrait phone.

### 18.3 Panes

A second, simpler docking system for app-level surfaces: Settings, Map, Packs, Search, Notes, and Commentaries. These open left, right, or bottom. On a phone they open wider (75% of the screen) than on a larger display (40%), since a narrow pane on a phone is unusable.

### 18.4 Rotation

The app is locked to portrait by default. Allowing rotation is a setting; when the screen turns, the app fades through the change rather than snapping.

## 19. Content Packs

Everything in the app — translations, lexicons, commentaries, the encyclopedia, maps, art, voices — comes as a **pack** you choose to install. Nothing is streamed; once a pack is on your device it works offline forever.

- **Quick install** — one tap for the common packs.
- **Advanced install** — install from a file or a URL, for packs you've made or been given.
- **Voice packs** — Read Aloud voices are managed here too.
- **See what's installed**, how big it is, and remove anything you don't want.
- **Database statistics** — how much space your content is using.
- **Packs load when needed** — the app fetches a pack the first time you use something that requires it, with a progress bar, rather than making you wait at startup.
- **Bundled packs** are extracted on first run so the app is useful immediately.

## 20. Account & Sync

An account is **entirely optional**. Everything works signed out; your data simply stays on that device.

### 20.1 Account

Create an account with an email and password. You can also change your name, change your password, reset a forgotten password, and delete your account outright.

### 20.2 What syncs

Signing in syncs your personal data across devices:

- Notes
- Notebooks and their pages
- Verse highlights and word highlights
- Saved verses
- Journal entries
- Reading plans
- Reading progress

Plus a **subset of settings** that should follow you: theme, your custom theme and the Notes and Journal themes, timezone, your default translations, interlinear preferences, red-letter, section headings, art icons, and themed titles.

Your wake alarm is stored with your account too — it has to be, since the alarm is sent from there.

### 20.3 What deliberately doesn't sync

Font size, line spacing, verse layout, word wrap, screen rotation, and update checks stay on each device. A phone and a desktop rarely want the same font size.

### 20.4 Working offline

- **Changes made offline are queued** and uploaded when you're back online. Nothing is lost.
- **Repeated edits to the same thing are collapsed** into one upload rather than replayed one by one.
- **Failed uploads retry** with increasing delays, up to fifteen minutes apart, so a flaky connection doesn't hammer the server.
- **Live updates** — with two devices open at once, a change on one appears on the other without a refresh.
- **Reading progress merges rather than overwrites.** A chapter ticked on your phone and a different chapter ticked on your tablet both survive; neither device wins by being last.
- **A fresh install won't wipe your account.** Settings only come down when the server's copy is genuinely newer than what this device last synced, so signing in on a new phone never overwrites your real settings with an empty one.

### 20.5 Profile

Your profile has four tabs — Reading Plan, Saved Verses/Notes, Journal, and Settings — plus today's reading, ready to tap.

## 21. App Settings & Appearance

Settings are grouped into five sections you can fold away — Appearance, Reader, Read Aloud, General, and Storage & Updates. Each shows a one-line summary of its own contents while closed, so you can find what you're after without opening all five.

**There's no Save button.** Every change takes effect the moment you make it.

### 21.1 Themes

Five options: **Dark**, **Light**, **Sepia**, **Auto** (follows your system), and **Custom**. Red-letter text is tuned separately for each theme so Jesus' words stay properly red rather than washing out.

### 21.2 Custom theme

Choose your own reading typeface, text color, and background color.

- **Twenty typefaces**, from clean serifs to handwriting.
- **Any two colors** you like, for text and background, with a live preview.
- **Save your favorites** — up to ten text colors and ten background colors, kept as swatches.

Only the Bible text changes. Buttons, book colors, and your highlight colors stay as they are, so the app stays familiar and your highlights keep meaning what they meant.

Switching typeface won't make the text jump. Each face is sized and spaced to match the others, so a change of font is a change of font and not an accidental change of size — and your font-size and line-spacing sliders keep working exactly as before.

### 21.3 Notes and Journal themes

The Notes and Journal writing surfaces get their own themes, separately from each other and from the reader. Notes can be one color scheme while the Journal is another. Color swatches are shared between all three, so a color you save in one place is available everywhere.

The formatting toolbar slides out of the way when you want the space.

### 21.4 Text

- **Font size** — 12 to 32 pixels.
- **Line spacing** — adjustable.
- **Verse layout** and **word wrap** — see [1.1](#11-the-reading-page).

### 21.5 Display

- Words of Jesus in red letters
- Theme colors in reader titles
- Art icons on Bible scenes
- Place-name underlines
- Which menu opens when you tap a word — the ring or the older popup
- Allow screen rotation

### 21.6 Read Aloud settings

Voice, speed, read section headings aloud, highlight the verse being read, the drifting glow, and the wake alarm. See [16](#16-read-aloud) and [22](#22-wake-alarm).

### 21.7 Time zone

Set your time zone so "today" means today where you are. This drives the journal, reading plans, streaks, and the daily greeting. Defaults to your device's time zone.

### 21.8 Updates

- **Automatic updates** — the app checks for a new version on open and whenever you come back to it, then updates itself and tells you it's running the latest version. Can be turned off.
- **Check for updates** manually at any time.
- **Clear cache** — wipes cached packs, service workers, and databases. The fix if packs won't install or the app is stuck on old data.

### 21.9 Daily greeting

The first time you open the app each day you get a greeting and a **Verse of the Day**, tappable to go straight there. The greeting knows the church calendar — Easter, Good Friday, and other moveable feasts are worked out for the actual year, along with Thanksgiving, so they land on the right day rather than a fixed guess.

### 21.10 Installing as an app

ProjectBible is a Progressive Web App. Install it from your browser and it runs like a native app — its own icon, full screen, and fully offline.

## 22. Wake Alarm

An alarm that wakes you with Scripture. Set a time, and the app opens on a passage.

- **Set the time and the days** — every day, or only the ones you choose.
- **Choose what it opens on** — carry on where you left off, a fixed chapter you pick, or the next day of your reading plan.
- **It works with the app closed.** A web app can't wake itself, so the alarm is sent to your phone the way any other notification is. That does mean it needs an account and an internet connection at the moment it's due.
- **What it opens on is decided when it rings**, not when it was set. Finish a chapter at 11pm and the 6am alarm reflects that.
- **Send a test alarm** to check the whole thing works without waiting for morning.

The sleep timer is the other half of this idea — see [16.4](#164-sleep-timer).

## 23. The Study Library

Four reference works you can **browse like books**, rather than only reaching them by tapping a word: the **Dictionary**, **Nave's Topical Bible**, the **Encyclopedia**, and **People**.

### 23.1 Four tabs, one card

All four works sit as tabs across the top of every lookup. They're always in the same order and always the same width, so a tab is in the same place every time. A tab is greyed out when that work has nothing on your subject — so a tab you can tap is one that will definitely open something.

Switching tabs doesn't close and reopen anything. Each work remembers where you were, so you can look something up in the Encyclopedia, check the Dictionary, and come back to find your place kept.

### 23.2 Browsing

- **An A–Z rail** down the side. Letters with no entries are dimmed rather than hidden, so the strip stays a steady ruler you can aim at.
- **Jump to any letter**, and see how many entries it holds.
- **Search inside one work** from its own search box.
- **Star entries** you want to keep, and see the ones you read recently.
- **Picks up where you left off** — reopen within half an hour and you're back on the same entry. After that it opens fresh, because an hour later is a new sitting.

All three lists work identically, so learning one teaches you the others.

## 24. Nave's Topical Bible

The classic topical index: pick a subject and get its outline and every verse under each point.

- **Browse every topic** A–Z, or search.
- **The outline** — the numbered structure of the subject, each point with its verses.
- **Every verse is tappable**, and colored by the book it comes from.
- **Bridged to the other works** — a topic connects to its encyclopedia article, its dictionary entry, and the people involved.

Included free with the encyclopedia pack.

## 25. People

Biographies of the people of the Bible.

- **Browse everyone** A–Z, or search by name.
- **Open a person from the text** by tapping their name.
- **When a name belongs to several people** — the Herods, the Marys — you're offered the list rather than being guessed at, and the one you pick is the one that stays open.
- **Family links** — parents, children, and spouses are links you can follow.
- **Pin a biography beside the reader** so you can keep reading with it open.
- **Bridged to the encyclopedia**, the dictionary, and the topical index.

## 26. Notes & Notebooks

A desk for everything you write, in its own panel.

### 26.1 Two kinds of notes

- **Verse notes** — everything you've attached to a verse, organised in a book-by-book tree.
- **Notebooks** — folders you name yourself, holding free-form pages that aren't tied to any verse.

Tap to open, edit full-width, and use Back to return to the list. The same shape works whether the panel is a narrow sliver or half the screen.

### 26.2 Bible references become links as you type

Write "Romans 8:28" in a note and it becomes a link.

- **It waits until you're finished.** Typing "Luke 1" won't link and rewrite itself while you're still typing the "2".
- **It corrects itself.** Fix "Luke 12:1" to "Luke 12:11" and the link follows. Break it into something that isn't a reference and it quietly turns back into ordinary text.
- **Print the verse inline** — expand a reference to show the verse text with it. The printed verse can't be edited, because a verse printed in a note should be the verse. Delete the reference and the verse goes with it.
- **Tap a link** to open a menu: go there, expand, or collapse.

Notes and notebooks sync to your account like everything else.
