Book picture lab v1.2

One book per page, its whole text set inside a symbol, or every book at once as Bubbles. Pinch, scroll or double-tap to zoom in. The text is the NET from the starter pack.

Word glow

Every verse with the word lights up as a dot in its chapter, placed the way the verses are: verse 1 in the middle and the rest spiraling out. Books without it fade, and each book shows how many verses have it. Tap a dot to read the verse. It matches by spelling, the way Repeats does.

Word clouds

Zoomed out, each book shows its own cloud: its most used words, sized by how often it uses them. Pinch in and the cloud gives way to the chapters. A lit word swaps the clouds for its dots. Tap a cloud word to light it up.

Book color is the book's own group color. Cream white is plain cream text with the first word of each chapter in the book color (with Mark chapter starts on). The others try a different group's color on this book.

Shape only: the text fills the symbol and nothing else. Whole page: the text fills the page, with the symbol's part in the color and the rest faint. Both: the two side by side. Word cloud: each of the book's words once, sized by how often the book uses it, packed into the symbol, with filler words (the, and, of…) left out. Tap a cloud word to light it up on All books.

Bold makes the symbol darker and easier to see from far away, at the cost of a little reading comfort up close.

Space between lines, as a multiple of the letter size. Tighter is a more solid symbol; looser is easier to read.

The first word of each chapter is drawn a little brighter (in the book color on cream white text), since there are no chapter or verse numbers.

Whole page look only: how strong the text outside the symbol is, and whether it is plain or a faint copy of the color.

Shape

On: the longest book fills the page and every other book's symbol is smaller to match how much text it has, so the letters are about the same size in every book and the symbol shows how big the book is. Off: every symbol fills the page, so a long book is a fine weave and a short one is big letters.

Empty space around the symbol, as a share of the page width.

Fattens every edge of the symbol. Thin parts (the anchor's arms, the quill's stem) hold only a word or two per line; this gives them room, mostly for short books.

Any piece of a line narrower than this many letters is left empty and the text jumps over it. Lower fills thin tips and edges; higher keeps squeezed words out.
