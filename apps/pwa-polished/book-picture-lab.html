Bold makes the symbol darker and easier to see from far away, at the cost of a little reading comfort up close.

Space between lines, as a multiple of the letter size. Tighter is a more solid symbol; looser is easier to read.

Shape

The page is a fixed size, so the letter size changes per book: a long book is a fine weave, a short one is big letters.

Empty space around the symbol, as a share of the page width.

Fattens every edge of the symbol. Thin parts (the anchor's arms, the quill's stem) hold only a word or two per line; this gives them room, mostly for short books.

Any piece of a line narrower than this many letters is left empty and the text jumps over it. Lower fills thin tips and edges; higher keeps squeezed words out.
