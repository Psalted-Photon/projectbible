book: Psalms

# Smaller stories are each psalm, titled with its BSB heading. Bigger stories
# are the collections inside each of the five books; titles are our own.
# "| Author · Kind" is the info line; "->" is the moment in David’s life the
# psalm’s heading names. Kinds: Cry for help, Praise, Thanks, Trust, Wisdom,
# The King, History, Pilgrim song, Confession, Mixed.

card: How Psalms Is Built
- Book I (1–41) · Nearly all David. Mostly cries for help from a man under pressure, with trust breaking through.
- Book II (42–72) · The sons of Korah, then David again. Longing, confession and the king, closing with “the prayers of David son of Jesse are ended.”
- Book III (73–89) · Asaph and the sons of Korah. Hard questions: the temple in ruins, and in Psalm 89 the promise to David’s line seems broken.
- Book IV (90–106) · Opens with Moses. The answer to Book III: kings may fail, but the LORD reigns.
- Book V (107–150) · Gathered home. Thanks, pilgrim songs on the road to Jerusalem, and a rising flood of praise.
> From cries to praise: cries for help crowd the first half of the book. Praise takes over toward the end, until the last five psalms are nothing but Hallelujah.
> “God” or “the LORD”: in Psalms 42–83 the writers mostly say “God” (Elohim) where the rest of the book says “the LORD” (Yahweh). Psalm 53 even repeats Psalm 14 with the name changed.
> Closing blessings: each of the five books ends with a short blessing of the LORD (41, 72, 89, 106), and Psalm 150 closes the whole book in praise. Many readers see an echo of the five books of Moses.
> The big arc, as many readers see it: God’s King is crowned (2), the kingdom falls (89), the people learn again that the LORD reigns (93–100), and they come home singing (107–150).
