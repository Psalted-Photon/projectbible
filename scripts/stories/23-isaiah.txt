book: Isaiah

card: How Isaiah Is Built
- 1–12 · Judah and Jerusalem: rebellion and judgment, Isaiah’s call, and the promise of Immanuel and a child to be born.
- 13–27 · The nations: messages against Babylon, Moab, Egypt, Tyre and more, then judgment on the whole earth.
- 28–35 · Woes and hope: don’t lean on Egypt; a king will reign in righteousness.
- 36–39 · History: Hezekiah, the Assyrian siege and the envoys from Babylon, also told in 2 Kings 18–20.
- 40–55 · Comfort: the LORD will bring his people home from Babylon, and his Servant will suffer for them.
- 56–66 · New creation: the nations stream to Zion, under new heavens and a new earth.
> Two halves: chapters 1–39 mostly warn of judgment in Isaiah’s own day. Chapter 40 opens with “Comfort, comfort My people” and looks past the exile.
> The Servant songs: four poems (42:1–9, 49:1–7, 50:4–11, 52:13–53:12) about the LORD’s Servant. The New Testament reads the last one as fulfilled in Jesus’ death.
> “The Holy One of Israel”: Isaiah’s favorite name for God, used about 25 times across both halves.
