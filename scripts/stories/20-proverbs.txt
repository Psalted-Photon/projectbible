book: Proverbs

card: How Proverbs Is Built
- 1–9 · A father’s teaching: longer poems, with Wisdom and Folly calling out like two women in the street.
- 10:1–22:16 · Solomon’s proverbs: 375 two-line sayings, most setting the wise against the foolish.
- 22:17–24:34 · Sayings of the wise: thirty sayings, then a few more.
- 25–29 · More of Solomon’s proverbs, copied by King Hezekiah’s men.
- 30–31 · The words of Agur and of King Lemuel, ending with the noble wife.
> The key: “The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge” (1:7).
> Proverbs, not promises: each saying shows how life usually goes. Sometimes two sit side by side and seem to disagree (26:4–5), because being wise means knowing which one fits.
> The last poem (31:10–31) is an acrostic: each verse starts with the next letter of the Hebrew alphabet.
